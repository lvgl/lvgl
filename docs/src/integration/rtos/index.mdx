## Overview

LVGL needs an operating system for exactly two things: threads, so rendering can be spread across
cores, and locking, so the UI can be touched from more than one task. Both are configured with
<ApiLink name="LV_USE_OS" />.

Everything else --- displays, input, rendering --- is independent of the RTOS. That means for most
RTOSes the integration is a one-line config change, and the interesting choice is which
[display driver](/main-modules/display_drivers) and [renderer](/main-modules/renderers) your
hardware calls for.

## What Each Integration Provides

| RTOS | `LV_USE_OS` | Display and input driver |
|---|---|---|
| [FreeRTOS](/integration/rtos/freertos) | <ApiLink name="LV_OS_FREERTOS" /> | From your hardware — an [LCD controller](/main-modules/display_drivers/lcd) or a [SoC controller](/main-modules/display_drivers/overview#soc-display-controllers) |
| [Zephyr](/integration/rtos/zephyr) | Not set by the Zephyr module | Zephyr's own display API, configured in the devicetree |
| [NuttX](/integration/rtos/nuttx) | <ApiLink name="LV_OS_PTHREAD" /> | **[NuttX driver](/main-modules/display_drivers/nuttx)** — `/dev/fb*` or `/dev/lcd*`, plus touch and mouse |
| [QNX](/integration/rtos/qnx) | <ApiLink name="LV_OS_PTHREAD" /> | **[QNX driver](/main-modules/display_drivers/qnx)** — QNX Screen, with keyboard and pointer |
| [RT-Thread](/integration/rtos/rt-thread) | <ApiLink name="LV_OS_RTTHREAD" /> | From your hardware, or RT-Thread's own drivers |
| [PX5](/integration/rtos/px5) | <ApiLink name="LV_OS_PTHREAD" /> — PX5 is POSIX | From your hardware |
| [MQX](/integration/rtos/mqx) | <ApiLink name="LV_OS_MQX" /> | From your hardware |

There is also `LV_OS_CMSIS_RTOS2` for any RTOS exposing the CMSIS-RTOS2 API, and `LV_OS_CUSTOM`
for wiring up one that is not listed.

<Callout type="info" title="NuttX and QNX are the exceptions">
Most RTOSes leave the display to you. NuttX and QNX are different: they expose an OS-level display
interface, so LVGL ships a driver for each. Those two driver pages live with the other
[display drivers](/main-modules/display_drivers); the pages in this section cover building LVGL
for the OS itself.
</Callout>

## Do You Even Need an RTOS?

No. LVGL runs perfectly bare metal with `LV_USE_OS` left at `LV_OS_NONE` --- call
<ApiLink name="lv_timer_handler" /> from your main loop and feed it a tick. Reach for an RTOS when
you want [multi-core rendering](/main-modules/renderers/sw#multi-core-rendering) or your
application already needs one for other reasons.

<Callout type="warn" title="Locking">
As soon as more than one task touches LVGL, every access must be wrapped in
<ApiLink name="lv_lock" /> / <ApiLink name="lv_unlock" />. LVGL is not thread-safe on its own.
</Callout>
